02 / Comparative evaluation

A/B image comparisons

One collection of briefs, paired assets, and written decisions. Five studies identify a preferred option; the sixth shows when both assets require revision.

6 studies
01Form & FieldPalette & toneProduct
Product campaign

A calm brand needs a calm image.

The brief. Use muted green and cream, an uncluttered composition, and a calm tone. Keep the product as the focal point. Avoid neon colors and promotional bursts.

  • Muted green and cream palette
  • Product-first visual hierarchy
  • No promotional bursts
Review decisionSelect option AConfidence: high

Evidence

The cream background and muted bottle follow the prescribed palette. Open space preserves the product silhouette, while the restrained headline supports the requested tone.

Alternative assessment

B uses neon pink and a prominent promotional circle. Those features violate explicit exclusions and compete with the bottle.

Actionable revision

Replace the pink background with cream, remove the burst, and preserve the product placement.

Before release

Confirm the small label is readable at the final placement size.

The palette and promotional exclusions are explicit.
02NorthlineHierarchy & readabilityCampaign
Digital campaign

One message. One clear next step.

The brief. Use navy, off-white, and orange. Show an outdoor trail, one dominant headline, and one clear call to action. Text must remain readable against the background.

  • One dominant headline
  • Visible call to action
  • Navy, off-white, and orange
Review decisionSelect option AConfidence: high

Evidence

A establishes one headline and separates the action in an orange field. The landscape remains visible without competing with the message.

Alternative assessment

B gives three slogans similar visual weight. The gray, unboxed action is less prominent against the dark landscape.

Actionable revision

Reduce B to one headline and place the action on a solid orange area.

Before release

Measure text contrast and inspect readability at mobile delivery size.

The headline count and action hierarchy are directly observable.
03Common GroundSubject & contextEditorial
Editorial feature

Match the story, not just the colors.

The brief. Create an editorial illustration for a community gardening feature. Use warm earth tones, show people actively gardening together, and leave room for a headline.

  • Visible collaborative activity
  • Flat editorial illustration
  • Warm earth tones
Review decisionSelect option BConfidence: high

Evidence

B shows two people planting together. Both assets use the approved style and palette, but only B satisfies the collaborative subject requirement.

Alternative assessment

A depicts plants alone. Its aesthetic fit does not resolve the missing human activity required by the story.

Actionable revision

Use B and place the headline in the open upper-left area without covering the people.

Before release

Review the final headline and crop together; protect faces and hands.

The required activity is present in only one option.
04Morrow CoffeeInformation integrityProduct
Product listing

Brand atmosphere must preserve product facts.

The brief. Use a warm, earthy palette and a tactile coffee-package illustration. The product name, origin, and roast level must remain visible. Decorative elements must not cover required information.

  • Visible origin and roast level
  • Warm, earthy palette
  • Unobstructed package label
Review decisionSelect option AConfidence: high

Evidence

A preserves the complete label, including Colombia and medium roast. Its warm background and brown packaging meet the mood and palette requirements.

Alternative assessment

B shares the same visual style, but its decorative seal covers the origin and roast information. A stylistic match cannot compensate for losing required content.

Actionable revision

Move the seal outside the label or remove it. Keep origin and roast information in a clear, dedicated area.

Before release

Verify the label against the approved product copy and inspect at final size.

The required label content is visibly obscured in B.
05Aster HouseComposition & placementEditorial
Hospitality feature

Give the architecture room to speak.

The brief. Use a warm neutral interior illustration with an unobstructed architectural focal point. Reserve a separate, quiet area for the headline. Avoid text crossing the central window.

  • Headline clear of the window
  • Warm neutral interior
  • Distinct text and image areas
Review decisionSelect option BConfidence: high

Evidence

B separates the copy from the interior. Its right-side window stays unobstructed, and the calm left column supports a measured reading order.

Alternative assessment

A puts the headline across the central window. The warm palette is consistent, but the text treatment fails the placement requirement.

Actionable revision

Keep B’s split composition and preserve the separation when adapting it to other aspect ratios.

Before release

Check a narrow crop separately; do not assume the horizontal composition will survive a vertical placement.

The brief specifies exactly where text must not appear.
06Luma AudioMandatory-rule failuresProduct
Product campaign

Sometimes the correct decision is revision.

The brief. Use only navy and cool neutrals. Show the complete headphones with both earcups inside the frame. Neither color treatment nor cropping may compromise those mandatory requirements.

  • Navy and cool neutral palette
  • Both earcups fully in frame
  • Complete product silhouette
Review decisionNeither option meets the briefConfidence: high

Evidence

Neither asset passes every mandatory rule. A shows the complete product but uses an unapproved magenta background. B follows the palette but cuts off the right earcup.

Why selection would be premature

Choosing solely by palette would conceal B’s crop failure. Choosing solely by completeness would ignore A’s color failure. Both need correction.

Actionable revision

Revise B: shift and scale the headphones so both earcups sit fully inside the canvas. Retain its navy and cool-neutral treatment.

Before release

Recheck the full silhouette and safe margins in every requested aspect ratio.

Each option has a visible failure against an explicit requirement.
03 / Evaluation framework

Criteria before preference.

A visually attractive asset can still be unsuitable. Review begins with mandatory requirements, then considers how effectively the asset communicates within those constraints.

These studies use qualitative decisions rather than unsupported performance scores.

01

Brand alignment

Check palette, typography, tone, and permitted styling against the supplied guidelines.

02

Subject & context

Confirm the image tells the requested story and suits its audience and placement.

03

Hierarchy & readability

Inspect the relationship between product, headline, supporting copy, and action.

04

Composition & integrity

Check focal points, protected content, safe margins, cropping, and completeness.

05

Release readiness

Separate the preferred option from final approval. Identify checks that still need the production asset.

04 / Process & documentation

A review that can be followed.

The decision should be easy for another reviewer or designer to understand, verify, and act on.

01

Read the brief

Extract requirements and exclusions. Separate mandatory rules from preferences and identify missing context.

02

Inspect both options

Use the same viewing size. Compare subject, visual system, content, composition, and technical limitations.

03

Record the evidence

Name the preferred asset and tie the choice to visible features. State why the alternative is less suitable.

04

Specify the next step

List revisions and release checks. Escalate ambiguity instead of inventing a missing brand rule.

About this portfolio

Abbas Haq

This portfolio brings together concept studies in visual brand evaluation, image selection, and structured review writing. It emphasizes consistent reasoning across different visual identities.

The work is organized around observable evidence: what the brief asks for, what an asset shows, and what needs to change.
