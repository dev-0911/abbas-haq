01 / Product visual · Form & Field

Calm, natural, considered.

The brief: Use muted green and cream, an uncluttered composition, and a calm tone. The product must remain the focal point. Avoid neon colors and promotional bursts.

Decision & evidence

Select A. Its cream field and muted bottle satisfy the palette rule. Open space around the bottle supports product focus and the quiet headline reinforces the requested tone.

Why the alternative fails

B introduces neon pink and a bright promotional circle. Both directly conflict with the brief, even though the product is clearly visible.

Revision & confidence

Replace B’s pink field with cream and remove the burst. Confidence: high for the stated rules. Check the small product label at the intended delivery size.

02Campaign creativeNorthline
02 / Campaign creative · Northline

Energy without visual noise.

The brief: Use navy, off-white, and orange. Show an outdoor trail, one dominant headline, and one clearly visible call to action. Maintain readable text against the background.

Decision & evidence

Select A. Both options use the prescribed colors and a trail, but A establishes one headline and a distinct orange action area. It better satisfies the hierarchy requirement.

Why the alternative fails

B’s three similarly sized slogans compete for attention. Its unboxed gray action text is less prominent against the dark landscape.

Revision & confidence

Keep one headline and place the action on a solid orange field. Confidence: high on hierarchy. Validate actual contrast ratios and mobile readability before release.

03Editorial selectionCommon Ground
03 / Editorial selection · Common Ground

A good image can still be the wrong choice.

The brief: For a community gardening feature, use a flat editorial illustration with warm earth tones. Depict people actively working together and preserve a clear area for a headline.

Decision & evidence

Select B. Both illustrations match the palette and medium. Only B depicts people planting together, meeting the central subject requirement.

Why the alternative fails

A is visually coherent but shows plants alone. Aesthetic quality does not compensate for missing the brief’s collaborative activity.

Revision & confidence

Retain B’s open upper-left area for the final headline. Confidence: high for subject selection. Check the final crop and headline placement together before approval.

02 / Review process

A consistent standard for every review.

01

Interpret the brief

Separate mandatory brand requirements from preferences. Identify the intended audience and placement.

02

Inspect the assets

Compare subject, palette, composition, readability, cropping, and technical quality at the same scale.

03

Document the decision

Connect each selection to a specific rule and visible evidence. Explain the alternative's limitations.

04

Resolve uncertainty

Flag missing context, request revisions, and distinguish a preferred option from a release-ready asset.

03 / Sample review note

Evidence behind the selection.

A short evaluation designed to make the decision and next action easy to understand.

Selection: Option A, Form & Field.

Rationale: The muted green and cream palette follows the brief. The uncluttered composition keeps the product prominent, while the restrained headline supports a calm tone.

Revision to B: Remove the neon background and promotional burst.

Final check: Confirm product-label readability at the intended display size.
